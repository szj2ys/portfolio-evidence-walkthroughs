Portfolio evidence walkthroughs

Six fictional artifact packets for manual interview practice

For candidates preparing a project or portfolio discussion

Show the artifact. Explain your contribution. Name the limit.

Six worked portfolio exercises turn concrete fictional materials into a two-minute walkthrough and evidence-based follow-up practice.

Choose a packet close to the kind of work you want to explain. Each contains an inspectable excerpt, diagram, or table; clear ownership boundaries; a timed outline; skeptical questions with reasoned answers; and a printable planning sheet. The materials are invented teaching examples, never real client data or experience to claim as your own.

Choose a different kind of evidence

  1. Walk through a code change and its test evidence

    Show the defect, the smallest relevant change, and what the tests actually establish.

  2. Walk through research notes and a traceable synthesis

    Separate what participants said, your interpretation, and the next question the evidence leaves open.

  3. Walk through a UX revision without inventing validation

    Show a concrete before-and-after interaction and distinguish a design rationale from a tested outcome.

  4. Walk through an operations handoff and its evidence

    Use a process map and two case traces to explain responsibility, waiting, and exception handling.

  5. Walk through a support article and its review trail

    Show how an article narrows the reader’s problem, offers a safe next step, and records what was checked.

  6. Walk through an experiment readout with a data-quality gap

    Explain the proposed question, inspect the event trace, and show why the packet does not support declaring a winner.

Use a packet in three passes

First, inspect the artifact without reading the suggested answers. Decide what it shows and what it leaves unknown. Second, use the two-minute outline while pointing to the relevant evidence. Third, ask a partner to challenge one claim and answer from the packet rather than adding an invented fact.

When adapting the exercise, replace the packet with work you are authorised to disclose. Keep your own contribution separate from colleagues’ work. A reproduction or fictional reconstruction can explain a method, but must be labelled as such. Do not turn a proposed design, review comment, or isolated test into a claim about a shipped outcome.

Print or practise offline

Download the seven HTML files and style.css together and open index.html. The exercises require no account, scripts, trackers, uploads, or live interview service. Use the browser Print command for a packet and its planning sheet. Temporary typed notes are not saved by these pages; copy them before leaving. Only optional related reading links need internet access.

The aim is a more inspectable explanation of work, not a score or hiring prediction. A truthful limitation is part of a useful walkthrough.

Published by the MakeInterview/MockLive team. This is a manual practice resource; the separate live AI interview service is currently paused.

All artifacts, people, data, and results in these exercises are fictional. Use your own true, authorised experience in an interview. These exercises do not assess hiring suitability or establish real-world outcomes.
