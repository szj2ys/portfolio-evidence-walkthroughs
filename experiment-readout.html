The walkthrough task

An experiment portfolio should reveal how you handled evidence that was not ready for a decision. This fictional packet concerns a clearer label on a draft-save button. It includes a proposed comparison, an event definition, a tiny trace excerpt, and a readout note that withholds a result. The exercise is about evidence provenance and a quality gate, not calculating a persuasive uplift. The numbers are small teaching identifiers and counts, not a real experiment or a statistical sample. A useful walkthrough can show why you refused to overstate a result and what you asked the team to verify next.

The fictional artifact packet

E1 defines the question as whether a clearer button label helps eligible editors complete a draft-save task. E2 says an exposure event should precede a completion event for the same assigned session. E3 is a fictional trace excerpt with three sessions per condition. One variant session has a completion timestamp before its recorded exposure. E4 is the readout decision: hold interpretation until the event-order issue is understood. Assignment integrity, representativeness, and statistical uncertainty are not established by this packet.

E1 · Proposed question

Compare the existing “Save” label with “Save draft” for eligible editors on the draft page. Keep the underlying save action unchanged. Do not interpret an outcome until assignment and event definitions have been checked.

E2 · Event definition

exposed_at records when the assigned label is shown. completed_at records the task completion for that same assigned session. Under this definition, completion should not precede exposure. A missing completion is recorded as “none” in this teaching excerpt.

Personal contribution boundaries

The fictional candidate wrote the event-definition checklist, reviewed the trace excerpt, and raised the V2 inconsistency in E4. A product partner proposed the wording change. An engineer owns the event emission and timestamp source. The candidate did not establish random assignment, choose a complete statistical analysis, or ship a winning variant. Their contribution is a traceable quality concern and a bounded next investigation. Keep that claim distinct from “I ran a successful experiment.” The packet also does not establish that the label caused the recorded task completions.

A two-minute walkthrough

The intervals below total 120 seconds. Point to an artifact as you speak; the outline is a rehearsal guide, not a script to memorise or a claim that the fictional experience is yours.

  1. 0–20 seconds · State the question and candidate role

    Use E1 to describe the label comparison, then say that your contribution in this packet is the instrumentation check rather than a final outcome decision.

  2. 20–45 seconds · Explain the event contract

    Point to E2. Establish why event order matters before showing the problematic row, so the concern does not look like an arbitrary preference.

  3. 45–80 seconds · Inspect the trace

    Walk through V2 and compare it with V1. The issue is a completion before exposure under the stated definition, not merely a lower or higher completion count.

  4. 80–105 seconds · Show the bounded readout

    Read E4’s decision to withhold interpretation. Explain the questions for the engineer: timestamp source, session linkage, and scope of the inconsistency.

  5. 105–120 seconds · Close with what remains unknown

    “I would not remove V2 just to produce a cleaner result. The cause and a documented handling rule must come before an outcome claim.”

Skeptical follow-ups and reasoned answers

Why not drop the bad row and report the rest?

The packet does not establish that V2 is the only affected record or that exclusion would preserve a valid comparison. E4 asks for the scope and cause first. Removing an inconvenient row after seeing it could conceal a systematic issue rather than repair the evidence.

Could the event order simply reflect clock differences?

Yes, different clocks or delayed logging are possible explanations. The packet does not identify the actual cause. That is why E4 asks the engineer to verify timestamp sources and linkage. A possible explanation is not enough to declare the data trustworthy.

Does the clearer label appear to have more completions?

The excerpt contains two recorded completions in the clearer condition and one in the existing condition, but one clearer-condition record violates the event-order expectation. The excerpt is tiny and incomplete as experiment evidence. I would name the visible counts only to describe the table, not use them to claim effectiveness.

What did you deliver if there was no winner?

The fictional candidate delivered the event-definition checklist, identified a concrete trace inconsistency, and documented why interpretation should wait. Those artifacts help the team ask a testable engineering question. They do not guarantee a later correction or successful experiment.

Try the exercise, then adapt it honestly

Give a partner E3 without E2 and ask what information they need before interpreting the timestamps. Then reveal the event definition and rehearse a thirty-second explanation of why V2 deserves investigation. For your own portfolio, choose an authorised readout that includes a data-quality question, a null result, or an unresolved limitation. Show how you connected the concern to the underlying event contract. Avoid presenting a dashboard screenshot without the definitions that make its numbers interpretable.

What this packet cannot establish

This packet contains six invented session traces, no complete dataset, no verified assignment procedure, no uncertainty analysis, and no evidence about longer-term effects. It is not a template for declaring statistical significance or selecting an experiment winner. A real readout would require an appropriate design, analysis plan, and validated data. The portfolio exercise is narrower: explain the quality gate you owned, the evidence behind a concern, and the remaining work without hiding the absence of an outcome claim.
